# SEMA Market Research Brief

**Status:** Initial research brief  
**Research date:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** Build evidence for SEMA's business plan, market-entry choice, positioning, and early production roadmap.

## Product definition

SEMA is a conversational AI Business Advisor. It connects to business data so owners and managers can ask questions in natural language and receive explanations, KPIs, tables, charts, and specific recommended actions.

SEMA is not positioned as a dashboard or text-to-SQL assistant. SQL generation is internal. The intended outcome is to understand what is happening in the business, why, and what to do next.

The current MVP uses a synthetic ecommerce business to demonstrate the product end to end. Ecommerce is a test domain and possible entry vertical, not a permanent product boundary.

## Decisions to inform

1. Which segment combines strong pain, readiness, ability to pay, and reachable distribution?
2. Should SEMA enter through ecommerce, another vertical, or a horizontal SMB proposition?
3. Which products and workflows compete for the same job and budget?
4. Is Hebrew or integration with Israeli systems a wedge, table stake, or weak differentiator?
5. Which integrations are required for the first segment?
6. What pricing and onboarding model are plausible?
7. What are defensible TAM, SAM, and three-to-five-year SOM ranges?
8. Which assumptions require interviews or experiments rather than more desk research?

## Initial ICP hypotheses

Treat all three as hypotheses.

### Primary: digitally active Israeli SMBs

- Small and medium-sized Israeli businesses with meaningful digital operations
- Data across several operational, commerce, marketing, accounting, or BI systems
- No mature internal analytics function, or insufficient analyst capacity
- Buyer: owner, CEO, COO, business-unit, ecommerce, or marketing leader
- Recurring need to understand performance and decide what to do next

### Alternative A: ecommerce-first

- Israeli or internationally oriented ecommerce businesses
- Data across commerce, marketing, web analytics, payments, fulfillment, and accounting
- Advantage: common metrics and integrations can enable repeatable onboarding
- Risk: narrower market and established vertical competitors

### Alternative B: analyst-enabled mid-market teams

- Organizations already using a warehouse or BI platform
- Managers want self-service answers while analysts retain governance
- Advantage: cleaner data and higher willingness to pay
- Risk: longer sales cycles, stronger security requirements, and incumbent BI copilots

## Geographic scope

- **Primary deep dive:** Israel
- **Global benchmark:** North America, Europe, and globally available SaaS
- Include Israeli-founded global companies when overlap is relevant.
- Search in Hebrew and English.

## Required market map

Map four rings:

1. Direct AI Business Advisor products
2. Conversational BI, augmented analytics, and AI analytics
3. Vertical analytics and advisory products
4. Substitutes: BI, spreadsheets, general AI, analysts, consultants, agencies, manual reporting, and doing nothing

Scan 15-30 candidates, then conduct sourced deep dives on the 5-8 most consequential alternatives.

## Israel-specific questions

- Which direct or adjacent solutions are Israeli or actively sold in Israel?
- Which genuinely accept and answer business questions in Hebrew?
- Which provide Hebrew UI, correct RTL, and mixed Hebrew-English understanding?
- Which have documented connections to common Israeli systems?
- Are connections native, partner-built, generic routes, or custom projects?
- Which local BI firms, integrators, consultants, or agencies are substitutes?
- Do language, support, data residency, trust, implementation, or procurement create an entry advantage?

Do not freeze the integration list in advance. Begin with Priority, SAP Business One, Hashavshevet, Rivhit, iCount, Morning, monday.com, Wix, Shopify, WooCommerce, Google Analytics, Google Ads, Meta Ads, payment providers, and common databases or warehouses; expand based on evidence about the selected ICP.

## Competitor questions

For every shortlisted product determine:

- Target customer, buyer, use cases, geography, and positioning
- What the product does and how users reach value
- Data connection and onboarding model
- Whether it explains causes and recommends actions or mainly retrieves and visualizes
- Proactive versus user-initiated behavior
- Trust, validation, governance, and source transparency
- Hebrew capabilities and Israeli integrations
- Pricing, packaging, trial, implementation, and service component
- Customer and adoption evidence
- Similarities and differences relative to SEMA
- Why a buyer would choose it, SEMA, a substitute, or no change

## Market-sizing scope

Build separate ranges for:

- The primary Israeli ICP
- An ecommerce-first Israeli entry segment
- A broader international opportunity only when defensible data exists

Use bottom-up, top-down, and value-based methods with conservative, base, and upside scenarios. Show formulas and assumptions, and distinguish company population from product-qualified and commercially reachable customers.

## Evidence requirements

- Prefer primary and current sources.
- Cite every material claim beside the claim and include access dates.
- Label evidence as Verified, Corroborated, Inferred, Claimed, or Unknown.
- Date prices, features, integrations, customers, and market statistics.
- Record unsuccessful searches for strategically important claims.
- Never infer Hebrew support from an LLM or a named integration from a generic API.
- Never invent private revenue, market share, pricing, customers, or capabilities.

## Stopping rule and limitations

Broad discovery ends after all planned category and bilingual query families have been searched and two consecutive variations produce no material new candidate. Deep research ends when every important comparison field is supported or explicitly Unknown.

The report must state what was searched, what was not verified, and which conclusions need demos, vendor outreach, interviews, or commercial experiments.

## Out of scope for the first run

- Modifying SEMA product code
- Choosing a final business model without customer validation
- Treating market-size estimates as forecasts
- Full financial projections beyond opportunity and indicative pricing
- Claiming product-market fit from desk research
